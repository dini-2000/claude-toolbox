## 01
# Why the shore needs its mangroves back

<!-- notes: The north shore lost most of its mangroves to shrimp ponds in the 1990s. The ponds closed fifteen years ago, but the trees never came back by themselves because the old pond walls block the tide. -->

---

###### What a mangrove does
## Three jobs, one tree

### Nursery for fish
Young fish and crabs shelter among the roots until they are big enough for open water.

### A wall against the sea
A belt of trees slows storm waves and holds the mud in place when the monsoon swell comes in.

### Carbon in the mud
Mangrove soils lock away more carbon per hectare than most forests on land, and keep it for centuries.

<!-- notes: Fishers on the north shore tell us the crab catch near the old ponds is a fraction of what it was. That is the main reason the fishing society joined. -->

## 02
# What we did in 2026

<!-- notes: The season ran from March to September, between the two monsoons, when the mud is firm enough to walk on. -->

---

###### The season
## From seed pods to the shore

- **14,200** seedlings planted
- **6.5 ha** of shore replanted
- **340** volunteers on 23 planting days
- **9** schools in the nursery programme

<!-- notes: The seedlings came from seed pods collected from the surviving stands at the lagoon mouth. Each one spent about four months in the nursery before planting. -->

---

###### Overall
**71%**

of the seedlings were alive after six months, against the 60% we planned for.

<!-- notes: Most losses came in the first six weeks, from barnacles and from seedlings washed out before their roots took hold. -->
