Summary

In our first full season, 340 volunteers planted 14,200 mangrove seedlings along 6.5 hectares of the lagoon's north shore. Six months later, 71% of them were alive, well above the 60% we planned for.

Survival depended on one thing more than any other: how freely the tide reached the plot. Where an old shrimp-pond wall still blocks the water, fewer than six in ten seedlings survived. For 2027 we will open the last pond wall before we plant behind it, double the nursery and plant 9 hectares.

The season

Seed pods and the nursery

Volunteers collected seed pods from the surviving trees at the lagoon mouth in February and March. Each pod went into a bag of lagoon mud at the nursery shed, and the school beds raised a third of them. After about four months the seedlings stood 40 to 60 centimetres tall, with four to six leaves, and were ready to plantSeedlings planted shorter than 40 centimetres were washed out more often in the 2025 trial rows..

Planting days

We planted on 23 Saturdays at low tide, in rows a metre apart. Each volunteer planted about 40 seedlings in a morning. The fishing society lent boats for the two sites that cannot be reached on foot.

Most losses came in the first six weeks, before the roots took hold.Plot counts, November 2026

Survival by site

Volunteers counted every row on each site in November, six months after the last planting day.

The School shore and Old pond wall plots get the tide twice a day. The North spit plot sits behind a pond wall we have not yet opened, so the tide reaches it only on spring tides, and the mud there dries and cracks in the hot months. At the Canal mouth, barnacles settled on many young stems and pulled them over.

Money

The season cost Rs 2.86 million against a budget of Rs 3.0 million. Grants paid for most of it, and the fishing society gave its boats and fuel.

The plan for 2027

  • Open the North spit pond wall before the rains, once the divisional office grants the permit we applied for in October.
  • Grow 30,000 seedlings in the nursery, twice this year's number.
  • Plant 9 hectares, starting at the canal mouth, with barnacle checks every two weeks for the first two months.
  • Bring two schools on the south shore into the nursery programme.

Planting days stay on the first Saturday of every month. Everyone is welcome.
