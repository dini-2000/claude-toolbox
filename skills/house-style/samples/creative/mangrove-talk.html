Mangrove Return: the first season

What we planted on the north shore, what survived, and the plan for 2027

5 December 2026Lagoon nursery shed

This covers the planting season from March to September 2026 and the six-month survival counts we finished in November. Every figure comes from the nursery log and the plot counts volunteers made by hand.

01

Why the shore needs its mangroves back

The north shore lost most of its mangroves to shrimp ponds in the 1990s. The ponds closed fifteen years ago, but the trees never came back by themselves because the old pond walls block the tide.

What a mangrove does

Three jobs, one tree

Nursery for fish

Young fish and crabs shelter among the roots until they are big enough for open water.

A wall against the sea

A belt of trees slows storm waves and holds the mud in place when the monsoon swell comes in.

Carbon in the mud

Mangrove soils lock away more carbon per hectare than most forests on land, and keep it for centuries.

Fishers on the north shore tell us the crab catch near the old ponds is a fraction of what it was. That is the main reason the fishing society joined.

The seedlings came from seed pods collected from the surviving stands at the lagoon mouth. Each one spent about four months in the nursery before planting.

Six months on

Survival was best where the tide reached freely

The North spit plot sits behind a pond wall we had not yet breached, so the tide only reached it on spring tides. The School shore plot gets the tide twice a day.

Overall

71%

of the seedlings were alive after six months, against the 60% we planned for.

Most losses came in the first six weeks, from barnacles and from seedlings washed out before their roots took hold.

Open the pond walls first, then plant.

This is the main lesson of the season, and it changes the order of work for 2027.

03

The plan for 2027

The plan has two parts: opening the last pond wall, and planting more hectares with the larger nursery.

Next season

What changes

  • Nursery doubled to 30,000 seedlings
  • Fishing society trained to count plots
  • Breach the North spit pond wall before the rains
  • Plant 9 hectares, starting at the canal mouth
  • Add two schools on the south shore

The pond wall needs a permit from the divisional office. The application went in during October, and we expect an answer in January.
